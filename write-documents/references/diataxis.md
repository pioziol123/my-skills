# Applying Diátaxis

## Abstract

Use Diátaxis to match content to the reader's immediate need: learning through practice, accomplishing a task, finding precise information, or understanding a subject. These instructions adapt the framework to this skill's broader document scope and preserve the required outline, abstract, conclusion, sufficient context, and selective use of links.

## 2.1. Identify the need before selecting a form

After establishing purpose and audience, ask:

- Should the content primarily guide action or provide knowledge?
- Is the reader acquiring skills and understanding, or applying existing competence in their work?

| Reader's situation | Form | Author's primary obligation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learning through action | Tutorial | Provide a coherent practical learning experience |
| Accomplishing a concrete task | How-to guide | Help achieve an outcome in real conditions |
| Looking up information for work | Reference | Make precise answers easy to locate and interpret |
| Developing understanding | Explanation | Reveal context, relationships, reasons, and consequences |

Classify the need in this situation, not the reader's profession or overall seniority. The same reader may need a tutorial in one area and reference material in another. A filename or the word “documentation” does not establish the form. Ask the user if uncertainty about the need materially changes the content.

Source: [The Diátaxis compass](https://diataxis.fr/compass/).

## 2.2. Tutorial: learning through practice

Design a concrete exercise through which the reader encounters relevant actions and concepts. Distinguish the exercise's visible outcome from the competence it is intended to develop.

- Establish the starting point, materials or environment, and visible final result.
- Select one coherent, achievable path. Make choices on the learner's behalf when evaluating alternatives would require knowledge they are only beginning to acquire.
- Use steps that produce observable progress early and regularly. Describe expected results and what the reader should notice.
- Supply the context needed to perform and interpret each step. Keep extended theoretical discussion outside the exercise's main flow.
- Where useful, make actions repeatable so the learner can observe their effects again.
- Check the entire path from the declared starting point. Explicitly identify anything you could not verify.

Quality criterion: a reader with the stated prerequisites can follow the exercise, recognize progress, and reach the promised outcome.

Source: [Diátaxis: Tutorials](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials/).

## 2.3. How-to guide: accomplishing a task

Organize the document around the reader's concrete goal. Describe the actions and judgments needed to reach it.

- Name the task and state the situations in which the guide applies.
- Specify relevant prerequisites, required inputs, and assumed competence. Supply task-specific context without reteaching knowledge the audience already has.
- Order actions by dependencies and the natural flow of work.
- Account for consequential variations in real situations. Give the condition and consequence for each necessary branch.
- Where judgment is required, provide decision criteria. “Choose the appropriate option” is insufficient guidance.
- Explain how to verify the result and respond to problems that matter for completing the task.
- Retain explanations needed for correct action. Keep broader theory and exhaustive option catalogs outside the main procedure when they are needed at all.

Quality criterion: the reader can apply the guide to their situation, make the required decisions, and determine whether the goal was achieved.

Source: [Diátaxis: How-to guides](https://diataxis.fr/how-to-guides/).

A tutorial and a how-to guide can contain similar steps but serve different needs. For a tutorial, the author designs a learning situation. For a how-to guide, the author helps the reader navigate a situation arising from their work. The distinction is not simply beginner versus expert or long versus short.

Source: [Tutorials and how-to guides](https://diataxis.fr/tutorials-how-to/).

## 2.4. Reference: finding precise information

Organize material so readers can consult individual entries without reading the whole document from the beginning.

- Define the scope and provide complete information within it.
- Follow the conceptual structure of the subject, system, or domain being described.
- Use consistent entry patterns and terminology for comparable elements.
- Describe properties, permitted values, conditions, constraints, relationships, and behavior as relevant to the subject.
- Be precise and unambiguous. Expose knowledge gaps instead of presenting unsupported certainty.
- Include short examples when they clarify meaning or correct use.
- Provide the local context required to interpret an entry. Keep extended rationale and guided exercises separate so they do not obstruct lookup.

Quality criterion: the reader can quickly find the required information, interpret it correctly, and identify the conditions under which it applies.

Source: [Diátaxis: Reference](https://diataxis.fr/reference/).

## 2.5. Explanation: developing understanding

Build a coherent account around a bounded question or subject that needs to be understood.

- Identify what needs explaining and establish the limits of the discussion.
- Introduce necessary context, then connect concepts, mechanisms, and effects.
- Explain causes, decision rationales, and constraints that shape the situation.
- Consider alternatives, tradeoffs, and different perspectives when they improve understanding.
- Distinguish facts from interpretations and judgments. Do not invent historical rationales or people's intentions.
- Use examples, analogies, or short narratives when they reveal a meaningful relationship. State significant limits of an analogy where it could mislead.
- Maintain the progression of the explanation. Include detailed procedures and catalogs only to the extent required for understanding.

Quality criterion: the reader understands the subject together with its relevant reasons, relationships, and consequences.

Source: [Diátaxis: Explanation](https://diataxis.fr/explanation/).

## 2.6. Apply the framework proportionally

The following rules adapt Diátaxis to this skill. The framework itself recommends using its map to improve documentation rather than treating all four forms as a structure to fill. Its preference for iterative development does not override this skill's requirement to prepare an outline before drafting.

- When a document serves several needs, identify the function of each section and make transitions clear. One file can contain explanation, instructions, and reference entries when that arrangement serves the reader.
- Split material into separate documents when doing so improves use. Do not automatically create four files, four mandatory sections, or empty scaffolding.
- Preserve context essential to understanding the current passage. Link to useful expansion when appropriate; do not replace an essential explanation with a pointer.
- Retain purpose-specific structures for reports, specifications, proposals, decision records, and other genres. Use Diátaxis to clarify the function of their content without forcing the entire document into one category.
- For agent-facing documents, also identify the need: executing a procedure, consulting rules, or understanding context. Select the appropriate structure and level of detail. Do not assume that all agent-facing material is a how-to guide.
- Include the abstract and conclusion required by the main skill, adapting their length and content to the document's function.

Source for the framework's iterative approach: [Diátaxis as a guide to work](https://diataxis.fr/how-to-use-diataxis/).

## 2.7. Review alignment with the selected need

After drafting, return to the need identified in section 2.1. Check the actual content against the quality criterion for the chosen form.

Look for missing guidance, decision criteria, precise information, or explanatory connections. When a passage serves a different need from the surrounding material, deliberately decide whether to retain it, separate it, move it, or remove it. Make that decision according to the document's purpose and the reader's convenience.

## Conclusion

Use the framework to choose and evaluate how content serves its reader. The desired result is a usable document whose form supports its purpose, with enough context to understand and apply it.
